# Defining genetic mechanisms of insulin resistance that shape fat partitioning and cardiometabolic disease risk

Repository to showcase the preliminary results for the project proposed for Danish Diabetes and Endocrine Academy (DDEA)'s postdoctoral fellowship of 2026.

## Preliminary results section as it is in the grant application: 

In our recent work, we computed a multi-trait GWAS of fasting insulin, triglycerides, and HDL cholesterol (n ≤ 1.25 million)11,12 and identified 282 IR-associated loci. Consistent with the adipose expandability model, these loci were associated with reduced subcutaneous fat storage, increased visceral and ectopic fat accumulation and an adverse cardiometabolic profile. However, stratification of IR loci revealed genetically distinct groups of variants with divergent effects on fat distribution and regulatory activity during adipogenesis. These results provide strong support for the central hypothesis that IR comprises multiple biologically distinct subtypes that differ in their effects on fat distribution, tissue biology, and cardiometabolic disease risk.10 
To extend these observations, we performed preliminary joint analyses of an IR proxy trait (TG/HDL ratio; N=402,398)13 together with MRI-derived measures of adipose depot volumes and ectopic fat accumulation15,16. Using PLACO+17, we identified approximately 300 independent loci (r2 <0.01 in 1+/-Mb) jointly associated with IR and fat distribution traits (Pjoint < 5x10-8). Clustering analyses identified five genetic subgroups with distinct patterns of regional adiposity. The following analyses focus on the biological differences among the three subgroups exhibiting tissue-specific regulatory enrichment.

The largest subgroup (122 loci) displayed the classical signature of impaired adipose expandability, characterized by lower total and gluteofemoral fat mass together with increased visceral and ectopic fat accumulation. In contrast, other subgroups showed markedly different phenotypic profiles, including a subgroup (56 loci) associated with greater gluteofemoral fat storage and lower visceral adiposity, and another subgroup (84 loci) associated with reduced skeletal muscle fat infiltration but increased waist circumference and diabetes risk (Figure 1). Consistent with their divergent phenotypes, the subgroups showed distinct enrichments for variants overlapping active enhancers27 in adipose tissue, liver, and skeletal muscle, respectively (FDR<0.05) (Figure 2).
